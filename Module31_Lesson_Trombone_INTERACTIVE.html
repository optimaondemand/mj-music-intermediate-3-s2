🎺 PART 2 · MEET THE TROMBONE

The trombone is part of the brass family. Like other brass instruments, the player makes a buzzing sound with the lips into a cup-shaped mouthpiece. But the trombone is different in one big way.

Most brass: trumpets and tubas change pitch with valves, small buttons the player presses.
Trombone: changes pitch with a slide, a tube the player pushes out and pulls back in. Sliding the tube gives the trombone its smooth, sliding sound.

Watch and listen to how the trombone sounds and how the slide moves.

📚 PART 3 · READING WITH ACCIDENTALS

An accidental is a symbol that changes a note for a moment. There are three you should know. A sharp raises a note one half step. A flat lowers a note one half step. A natural cancels a sharp or a flat and brings the note back to where it started.

When you read a passage with accidentals, look just to the left of a note. If you see one of these three symbols, change the note before you play or say it.

▶  Go Further: listen for the slide
The next time you hear a band or a brass group, listen for a low voice that seems to glide between notes instead of jumping. That gliding sound is the trombone moving its slide. See if you can pick it out by ear.
