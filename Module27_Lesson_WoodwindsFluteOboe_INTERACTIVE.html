What makes a woodwind a woodwind, and how do the flute and the oboe sound different?

🎵 PART 2 · THE WOODWIND FAMILY

Woodwind instruments make sound when air moves through a tube. The player changes the notes by covering and uncovering holes. Two woodwinds you should know are the flute and the oboe.

Flute: bright and high; made of metal; the player blows across a hole near the top, the way you blow across a bottle.
Oboe: reedy and slightly nasal; the player blows through a double reed, two thin pieces of cane that vibrate together.

Watch and listen to how the flute sounds and how it is played.

📚 PART 3 · PROKOFIEV AND "PETER AND THE WOLF"

The composer Sergei Prokofiev wrote a story-piece called "Peter and the Wolf." He gave each character its own instrument so listeners can follow the story by ear. When you hear the high, bright flute, the bird has arrived. When you hear the reedy oboe, the duck is near.

Watch and listen to how Prokofiev matches instruments to characters.

🤗 NEED HELP?
Not sure how to tell the flute and the oboe apart? Use the fact cards above and listen again. The flute is bright and high; the oboe is reedy and a little nasal. Still stuck? Message your teacher in your Canvas Inbox.
▶  Go Further: listen for the other characters
As you listen to "Peter and the Wolf" again, try to catch the clarinet (cat), the bassoon (grandfather), the French horns (wolf), the strings (Peter), and the drums and timpani (hunters). See how many you can name by ear before the narrator says them.
