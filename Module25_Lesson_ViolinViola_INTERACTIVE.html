A Closer Look: Violin and Viola
Lesson
🎻 AT A GLANCE · VIOLIN vs VIOLA
Violin
Size: the smallest string instrument
Pitch: the highest of the strings
Role: often plays the melody on top
Viola
Size: a little larger than the violin
Pitch: a little lower than the violin
Role: often fills in the warm middle sound
A bigger body makes a lower sound. The viola is slightly bigger, so it sounds slightly lower than the violin.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How are the violin and viola alike, and how are they different?
The two highest members of the string family look almost like twins. This week you compare the violin and the viola, hear how their sizes change their pitch, and review reading notes on the treble clef.

🎻 PART 2 · MEET THE VIOLIN AND VIOLA

The violin is the smallest string instrument and plays the highest notes. The viola is its slightly larger cousin. Because the viola has a bigger body, it sounds a little lower and a little warmer. Watch and listen for that difference.

🎼 PART 3 · READING ON THE TREBLE CLEF

The violin and viola often read music on the treble clef. Here is a quick reminder of the note names on the five lines and four spaces. The five lines spell E G B D F from bottom to top, and the four spaces spell F A C E.

TREBLE CLEF · LINE AND SPACE NOTE NAMES

Tip: the spaces spell the word FACE

Remember your note values too: a quarter note gets one beat, a half note gets two beats, and a whole note gets four beats. Watch this short review of reading on the treble clef.
