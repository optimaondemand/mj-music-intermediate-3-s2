Clarinet vs. bassoon, and what an interval is

Two more woodwinds join the family this week. The clarinet has a smooth, wide range. The bassoon is large and plays low and deep. You will also learn how to measure the distance between two notes, which we call an interval.

🎼
Clarinet
Single reed (one thin piece of cane). Smooth tone with a wide range from low to high. Made of dark wood with a flared bell.
🎼
Bassoon
Double reed (two pieces of cane). Large and long, so it plays low and deep. The lowest common woodwind in the orchestra.

🎵 PART 2 · THE CLARINET AND THE BASSOON

Both of these instruments are woodwinds, so they make sound when air moves through a tube and the player changes notes by covering and uncovering holes. They use reeds, but in different ways.

Clarinet: uses a single reed, one thin piece of cane against the mouthpiece. It has a smooth tone and a wide range, from low and warm to high and bright.
Bassoon: uses a double reed, like the oboe, but it is much larger. Because it is long, it plays low and deep, often the lowest woodwind voice in the orchestra.

📚 PART 3 · INTERVALS, THE DISTANCE BETWEEN NOTES

An interval is the distance between two notes. We count the notes from the first one to the second one, including both ends.

A 2nd moves to the very next note, like C up to D. A 3rd skips one note, like C up to E (you skip over D). Look back at the visual at the top of the lesson and trace each one with your finger.
