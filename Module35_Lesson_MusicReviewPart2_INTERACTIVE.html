📚 PART 2 · THE BIG IDEAS ABOUT READING MUSIC

Here is a short refresher on the reading skills you built this year.

🎵 The treble clef and the staff
Music is written on a staff of five lines and four spaces. The treble clef tells you to read those lines and spaces with the higher note names. It is also called the G clef because it curls around the line for G.
📉 Lines and spaces
The five lines, from bottom to top, are E G B D F. The four spaces, from bottom to top, spell F A C E. Once you know these, you can name any note that sits on a line or in a space.
🎹 Ledger lines
When a note is too high or too low to fit on the staff, we add short ledger lines above or below it. You keep counting the musical alphabet up or down to name the note.
♯ Accidentals: sharp, flat, natural
An accidental changes a note for a moment. A sharp raises a note one half step, a flat lowers it one half step, and a natural cancels a sharp or flat and brings the note back to plain.

▶  Go Further: a one-minute reading drill
Look at the staff map above. Start on the bottom line and name each line and space out loud as you climb: E, F, G, A, B, C, D, E, F. Then go back down. If you can do it without stopping, your reading has come a long way this year.
