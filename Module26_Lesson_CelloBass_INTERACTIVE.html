Strings III: The Cello and Double Bass
Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Which string instruments make the lowest, deepest sounds, and how are they played?
This week you meet the two lowest voices of the string family: the cello and the double bass. Both are larger than the violin and viola, and both reach down into low, rich tones.
🎻 MEET THE TWO LOWEST STRINGS
Cello
Size: large, about the height of a seated person
Pitch: low and warm
Played: sitting down, held between the knees, resting on a metal pin on the floor
Double Bass
Size: the biggest string instrument, taller than most players
Pitch: the lowest of all the strings
Played: standing up or on a tall stool, leaning the instrument against the body
The drawings show the difference in size: the double bass is even bigger than the cello.

🎻 PART 2 · THE CELLO

The cello is a large string instrument with a warm, low voice. A player sits down to play it, holding the cello between the knees while it rests on a metal pin on the floor. Its sound is often described as deep and singing.

Family: strings (played with a bow or plucked)
Size: large, about as tall as a seated player
Sound: low and warm
How it is played: sitting down, held between the knees

🎻 PART 3 · THE DOUBLE BASS

The double bass is the biggest and lowest string instrument. It is so tall that players usually stand up or lean against a high stool to play it. Its deep tones form the foundation under the rest of the orchestra.

Family: strings (played with a bow or plucked)
Size: the biggest string instrument of all
Sound: the lowest of the strings
How it is played: standing up or on a tall stool

🤗 NEED HELP?
Mixing up the cello and double bass? Remember: both are low strings, but the double bass is bigger and lower, and players usually stand to play it, while a cello player sits down. Still stuck? Message your teacher in your Canvas Inbox.
▶  Go Further: listen for low strings
Next time you hear orchestra or movie music, listen for the deep, low notes underneath the melody. Those are often the cellos and double basses holding up the whole sound. Try humming the lowest note you can sing and imagine how much lower a double bass can go.
