Comprehensive Review: A Whole Year of Music
Review Lesson
🎧 YOUR YEAR IN REVIEW

Look how much you learned this year. Everything fits into four big buckets. Read each card and say one thing you remember from it out loud.

🎻
Instruments
The four families: strings, woodwinds, brass, and percussion. Each family makes sound a different way and gives the orchestra its many colors.
🎧
Composers
The people who made the music: Mozart, Beethoven, Vivaldi, Prokofiev, Louis Armstrong, and Dave Brubeck. From classical to jazz.
🎵
Reading & Rhythm
Notes on the staff, the musical alphabet, note values, and time signatures. The skills that let you read and clap music.
👂
Ear Training & Form
Hearing major and minor, high and low, intervals, and the shape of a piece, like the AABA form in a jazz tune.
📖 THIS WEEK'S FOCUS
You made it through a whole year of music. Let's pull it all together.
This is a review week. There is no new topic. You will revisit the four buckets above, refresh the big ideas, and get ready to show everything you know. Take your time and notice how much you have grown.

🎻 PART 1 · INSTRUMENTS AND COMPOSERS

First, the people and the tools of music. Watch this quick tour of the four instrument families, then say which family your favorite instrument belongs to.

Video: instrument families of the orchestra. If it does not load, watch on YouTube.

🎻 The four instrument families
Strings make sound from vibrating strings (violin, cello). Woodwinds use air through a tube or reed (flute, clarinet). Brass buzz the lips into a metal mouthpiece (trumpet, trombone). Percussion is struck or shaken (drums, xylophone).
🎧 The composers you met
Mozart and Beethoven wrote famous classical music. Vivaldi painted the seasons in sound, and Prokofiev told a story with instruments in Peter and the Wolf. Louis Armstrong and Dave Brubeck shaped jazz, with Brubeck even writing in unusual time signatures.
👂 PART 2 · EAR TRAINING AND FORM

Now the listening skills. Watch this short look at musical form, then listen for the parts that come back and the parts that change.

Video: how musical form works. If it does not load, watch on YouTube.

👂 Ear training
This year you trained your ear to hear high and low pitches, major (bright, happy) and minor (darker, serious) sounds, and the distance between two notes, called an interval.
🎹 Musical form
Form is the plan, or shape, of a piece: which parts repeat and which are new. We label parts with letters. AABA means a section repeats, a new B section comes in, then the A returns. Many jazz standards use AABA.
✏️ PART 3 · REVIEW CHECK

Think about each one first, then choose the best answer. Use the cards and refreshers above to check your thinking.

Forgot one of the big ideas? Read the matching card or refresher above, then say it out loud in your own words. If you are still unsure about a concept, message your teacher in your Canvas Inbox and name the one you want to review.
⚡ PART 4 · LOOK BACK ON YOUR YEAR
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Looking back at the whole year, which bucket are you proudest of growing in? (There is no wrong answer.)
▶  Go Further: a full year listen
Pick one piece you love and listen four times. Each time, follow only one bucket: first name the instruments you hear, then think about who might have written it and in what style, then notice the rhythm and any repeated tune, then listen for the form and whether it sounds major or minor. Doing all four shows yourself how much you grew this year.
📝 ADD A WEEKLY ENTRY TO YOUR MUSIC JOURNAL

For A new word, pick one term you want to remember, like interval, form, or AABA, and write what it means in your own words.
