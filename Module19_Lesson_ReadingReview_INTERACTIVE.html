📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How fast can you name the notes on the staff?
You have read notes before. This week you make naming notes quick and sure. Then you put it all together by labeling every note on a short piece of music.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I can name notes on the treble staff quickly and surely.
2
I can read a short passage of notes by letter name.
3
I can annotate, or label, the note names on a score.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED
▶
Headphones or speakers
▶
Your Music Journal organizer, and something to write with
⚡ PART 1 · LET'S BEGIN
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you see a note on the treble staff right now, how quickly can you name it? (There is no wrong answer.)
🎵 PART 2 · REVIEW THE LINES AND SPACES

The treble-clef staff has 5 lines and 4 spaces. Look back at the picture above. The line notes from bottom to top are E G B D F. A helpful saying for the lines is Every Good Boy Does Fine.

The space notes from bottom to top spell a word you already know: F A C E. Once you know which way is up on the staff, you can name any note by its letter.

Some notes sit just above or below the five lines. We draw a tiny extra line called a ledger line to hold them. Middle C, for example, sits on a ledger line just below the staff.

🎤 PART 3 · READ A PASSAGE, THEN LABEL IT

Reading a passage means naming the notes in order, left to right, just like reading words. Try it with this familiar climb: the C-Major scale, C D E F G A B C. Say each letter out loud as you point to it.

To annotate, or label, a score means to write the letter name under or beside each note. Musicians do this to read tricky spots more easily. This week you will label a whole short piece.

⚡ TRY IT · NAMING NOTES
Pick the best answer for each one.
1. The space notes of the treble staff, from bottom to top, spell...
2. The line notes of the treble staff, from bottom to top, are...
3. A note sitting on the very bottom line of the treble staff is named...
4. To annotate a score means to...
🤗 NEED HELP?
Mixing up lines and spaces? Say the line saying out loud, bottom to top: Every (E) Good (G) Boy (B) Does (D) Fine (F). Then read the spaces as the word F A C E. Point to each note in the picture as you say its name. Still stuck? Message your teacher in your Canvas Inbox.
▶  Go Further: read notes below the staff
Once you can name the lines and spaces without stopping, try the notes that sit on ledger lines just below the staff, like middle C and the D right above it. Keep counting down from the bottom line E: the space just below it is D, and the ledger line below that is C. Naming these makes you a faster reader of a whole piece.
📝 ADD A WEEKLY ENTRY TO YOUR MUSIC JOURNAL
Open your Music Journal organizer and add a new Weekly Entry. For What I learned, write the line saying and the space word for the treble staff. For A new word, write what it means to annotate a score.

💪 READING THE PAGE

You reviewed the lines (E G B D F) and spaces (F A C E) of the treble staff and learned that to annotate means to label the notes. Reading music takes practice, so keep naming notes a little each week. The more you read, the easier the page becomes.
