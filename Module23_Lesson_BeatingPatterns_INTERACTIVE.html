🎧 THE THREE BEATING PATTERNS

These are the three conducting patterns for 2/4, 3/4, and 4/4. Beating means marking each beat with your hand. The first beat of every pattern always moves straight down. Follow the numbered beats below, in order, to trace the path your hand makes.

24
down, up
1 2 ↓ ↑
Beat 1 goes down, beat 2 goes back up. Two beats per measure.
34
down, out, up (a triangle)
1 2 3 → ↗ ↙
Beat 1 down, beat 2 out to the side, beat 3 back up. The path makes a triangle.
44
down, left, right, up
1 2 3 4 ↙ → ↗
Beat 1 down, beat 2 to the left, beat 3 to the right, beat 4 back up. Four beats per measure.

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How do you keep a steady beat and read at the same time?
This week you put the beating patterns to work. You will practice keeping a steady tempo, beat the patterns for 2/4, 3/4, and 4/4 from memory, and then combine reading notes with conducting the beat.

🎜 PART 2 · A STEADY TEMPO, EVERY TIME

The tempo is how fast or slow the beat goes. A good conductor keeps the tempo even, so beats do not speed up or slow down. The first beat, the downbeat, always moves straight down, which is why beat 1 is green in the diagram above.

Watch this video again to refresh the three patterns, then beat them with your own hand.

✋ STEADY-TEMPO WARM-UP (TRY IT OUT LOUD)
Count "1, 2, 3, 4" out loud at a slow, even speed, like a clock ticking. Beat the 4/4 pattern as you count: down on 1, left on 2, right on 3, up on 4. Do it four times without speeding up. Then try 3/4 (down, out, up) and 2/4 (down, up) the same even way.
✋ PART 3 · READING WHILE YOU CONDUCT

Now put two skills together. You will read notes on the staff and beat the pattern at the same time. The trick is to let your hand keep the beat while your eyes follow the notes. Say the note name on each beat as your hand marks it.

Start slow. If your hand and your reading drift apart, slow down until they line up again. Then answer the check below using the patterns at the top of the page.

✋ READING WARM-UP (TRY IT OUT LOUD)
Pick a short line of notes you can already read on the treble clef. Beat the 4/4 pattern slowly with one hand. On each beat, say the name of the note your eyes land on. Keep your hand even, even if you have to read more slowly. That is reading while conducting.
⚡ TRY IT · BEATING CHECK
Pick the best answer for each one. Use the patterns at the top of the page to check your thinking.
1. What does the word "tempo" mean?
2. Which way does the first beat of every pattern move?
3. Which pattern uses only down, up?
4. If your beat and your reading start to drift apart, what should you do?
🤗 NEED HELP?
Reading and beating at once feels hard at first. Do it in two steps: first beat the pattern alone until it is steady, then add the reading slowly on top. Say each motion out loud as you go. Still stuck? Message your teacher in your Canvas Inbox.
▶  Go Further: a challenge
Once 4/4 feels easy, try switching patterns without stopping. Beat four measures of 4/4, then four of 3/4, then four of 2/4, keeping the same steady tempo through all of them. Notice that beat 1 still goes down every single time.

💪 LEAD THE BEAT

You can now keep a steady tempo, beat the patterns for 2/4, 3/4, and 4/4, and read while you conduct. Holding the beat even while your eyes move takes self-control, the same focus you use to stay on task. Next time you hear music, conduct along and feel the downbeat fall on 1.
