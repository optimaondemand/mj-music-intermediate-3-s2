🎵 LOOK FIRST
The three accidentals

An accidental is a small symbol that changes a note for a moment. Here are the three you will use this week.

♯
Sharp
Raises a note one half step.
♭
Flat
Lowers a note one half step.
♮
Natural
Cancels a sharp or a flat.
🎺 TRUMPET FACT CARD
The trumpet is the highest brass instrument. Its sound is bright and bold. The player presses three valves and buzzes the lips to change the notes.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What makes the trumpet sound so bright, and what do a sharp, a flat, and a natural do to a note?
This week you meet the brass family and its highest voice, the trumpet. You also learn three accidentals that change a note for a moment: the sharp, the flat, and the natural.

🎺 PART 2 · THE TRUMPET

Brass instruments make sound when the player buzzes the lips into a mouthpiece. The trumpet is the highest brass instrument, and its tone is bright and bold. The player presses three valves to reach different notes.

Trumpet: bright and high; made of brass; the player buzzes the lips into a mouthpiece and presses three valves to change the notes.

📚 PART 3 · SHARPS, FLATS, AND NATURALS

A half step is the smallest step between two notes. An accidental moves a note by a half step, or cancels an earlier change. A sharp raises the note one half step. A flat lowers it one half step. A natural cancels a sharp or a flat, so the note goes back to normal.

Sharp: raises the note one half step (a little higher).
Flat: lowers the note one half step (a little lower).
Natural: cancels a sharp or a flat (back to the plain note).

▶  Go Further: find an accidental in real music
