🎧 THE THREE CONDUCTING PATTERNS

A conductor shows the beat with hand motions. The first beat of every pattern always moves straight down. Follow the numbered beats below, in order, to see the path your hand traces.

down, up

34
down, out, up (a triangle)

44
down, left, right, up

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How does a conductor show a group when to play?
You already know how to read a time signature. This week you take the lead. Conducting means showing the beat with hand motions so a whole group stays together. You will learn the patterns for 2/4, 3/4, and 4/4 and conduct a steady beat yourself.

🎤 PART 2 · WHAT A CONDUCTOR DOES

A conductor stands in front of a group and shows the beat with hand motions. The conductor keeps the steady beat, shows when to start and stop, and shapes the loud and soft. Players watch the hand to stay together, the way you tap your foot to a steady beat.

The first beat of every measure, called the downbeat, always moves straight down. That is why beat 1 is green in the diagram above. Watch this video to see a conductor at work.

✋ PART 3 · THE PATTERNS, ONE BEAT AT A TIME

Look back at the three patterns at the top of the page. Each one matches a time signature you already know:

2/4 is two beats: down, up. 3/4 is three beats: down, out, up, and the path makes a triangle. 4/4 is four beats: down, left, right, up. Watch this video, then try the patterns with your own hand.

✋ CONDUCT WARM-UP (TRY IT OUT LOUD)
Stand up and raise your hand. Count out loud and conduct 2/4: say "down" as your hand goes down on 1, "up" as it lifts on 2. Repeat four times. Then try 3/4 (down, out, up) and 4/4 (down, left, right, up). Keep a slow, steady beat so your hand and your counting stay together.

🤗 NEED HELP?
Losing track of a pattern? Say each motion out loud as your hand moves: "down, up" for 2/4, "down, out, up" for 3/4, "down, left, right, up" for 4/4. Slow down until your words and your hand match. Still stuck? Message your teacher in your Canvas Inbox.
