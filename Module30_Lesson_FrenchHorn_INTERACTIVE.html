The French horn is a coiled brass instrument with a warm, round tone. Below is a quick fact card, and a reminder of how a sharp, a flat, and a natural change a note.

🎺
French Horn
Family: Brass
Shape: a long brass tube coiled into a round loop
Tone: warm and round
How it is played: buzz the lips into the mouthpiece and rest a hand inside the bell
DID YOU KNOW
If you uncoiled a French horn, the tube would stretch to about the length of a school bus. The player slides a hand into the bell to soften and shape the sound.

🎺 PART 2 · THE FRENCH HORN

The French horn belongs to the brass family. Brass players make sound by buzzing their lips into a cup-shaped mouthpiece. The French horn's tube is coiled into a round loop, and the player rests one hand inside the bell to shape and soften the tone.

Tone: warm and round, often used for calm or noble melodies.
How it is played: the player buzzes the lips, presses valves to change notes, and keeps a hand in the bell.

📚 PART 3 · REVIEW YOUR ACCIDENTALS

An accidental is a symbol that changes a note. A sharp raises the note one half step. A flat lowers the note one half step. A natural cancels a sharp or a flat and brings the note back to plain. When you label a note, you write its letter name and then its accidental, like F sharp or B flat.
