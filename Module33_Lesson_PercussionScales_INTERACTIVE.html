🎵 PART 2 · THE PERCUSSION FAMILY

Percussion instruments make sound when you strike or shake them. Inside the family there are two groups. Pitched percussion plays specific notes you can name, like the xylophone and the timpani. Unpitched percussion makes a sound without a definite pitch, like the snare drum and the cymbals.

Pitched: the xylophone has wooden bars that each play an exact note, and the timpani can be tuned to a chosen pitch.
Unpitched: the snare drum and the cymbals make a sound with no definite note, so you cannot sing along to one exact pitch.

📚 PART 3 · THE MAJOR SCALE

A major scale is a set of eight notes that climb in one fixed pattern of steps. The pattern is Whole, Whole, Half, Whole, Whole, Whole, Half. When you start on C and follow that pattern, you get C, D, E, F, G, A, B, C, which is the C major scale. Look back at the step strip at the top of the lesson to see it in order.

🤗 NEED HELP?
Not sure if an instrument is pitched or unpitched? Ask yourself one question: can you sing one exact note along with it? If yes, it is pitched, like the xylophone. If it is just a knock or a crash, it is unpitched, like the snare drum. Still stuck? Message your teacher in your Canvas Inbox.
▶  Go Further: try the pattern from a new note
The major-scale pattern works from any starting note, not just C. Try starting on G and singing up using Whole, Whole, Half, Whole, Whole, Whole, Half. You will need one sharp to keep the pattern. See if you can hear that the scale still sounds major.
